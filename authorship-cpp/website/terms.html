1. About these terms

These terms apply to anyone who installs or uses CALSS (Code Authorship Likelihood Scoring System), a Windows desktop application developed by the CALSS thesis team, College of Computer Studies, University of Luzon. By using CALSS you agree to these terms and to the Privacy Policy. If you use CALSS on behalf of an institution, you confirm that you are authorized to do so.

2. Authorized, educational use

CALSS is provided for programming instructors and other authorized academic staff to review student C programming submissions for courses they teach or administer, on computers approved by the University of Luzon College of Computer Studies / the laboratory administrator. It is a thesis project, provided free of charge, and is not a commercial product.

3. Findings require human review

CALSS is a decision-support tool. Its similarity scores, authorship-consistency scores, flags and optional AI-written explanations indicate submissions that may deserve a closer look. They are not proof of plagiarism, cheating, collusion or any other misconduct, and they are not a determination about any student.

You agree that:

  • you will personally review the underlying submissions and evidence before acting on any CALSS result;
  • you will not impose a grade penalty, sanction or accusation based solely on a CALSS score, flag or AI explanation;
  • any academic-integrity concern will be handled through your institution's established procedures, including the student's opportunity to respond.

4. Limitations of automated similarity and authorship scoring

  • High similarity can be legitimate: shared starter code, instructor examples, textbook solutions, short or tightly constrained problems, or common idioms.
  • Low similarity does not prove independent work; code can be disguised or written with outside help.
  • Authorship-consistency scores depend on how many earlier submissions a student has. CALSS reports a reliability level; scores built from few submissions carry little weight.
  • A student's style can legitimately change as they learn.
  • CALSS analyzes C source files only, and file-name conventions affect how files are grouped and named.
  • Gemini explanations are generated text. They can be incomplete or wrong and must be checked against the scores and the code.

5. Your responsibilities for student information

Student submissions and names are personal information. When you use CALSS you are responsible for:

  • using it only for students in courses you are authorized to assess, and only for academic purposes;
  • following your institution's data-privacy and record-retention policies and applicable law (for example, the Philippine Data Privacy Act of 2012) — to be confirmed by the university;
  • using your own Windows account where possible, and removing your Google sign-in from shared accounts (see Privacy Policy, sections 10 and 12);
  • deleting downloaded submissions and reports when they are no longer needed;
  • deciding whether to use the optional Gemini feature, given that it sends student names and scores to Google (see Privacy Policy, section 8), and whether that is permitted by your institution;
  • not using real student work in demonstrations, screenshots or testing outside the course without authorization.

7. Prohibited use

  • use CALSS or data obtained through it for advertising, profiling unrelated to coursework, or sale;
  • attempt to extract, misuse or redistribute the CALSS OAuth client configuration or another person's stored sign-in;
  • modify or redistribute the CALSS installer in a way that misrepresents its origin.
